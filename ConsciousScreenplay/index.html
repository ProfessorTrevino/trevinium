Who it's for

Everything the page implies, and where it says so.

For anyone who needs to know a script deeper than what's written on the page, with every answer drawn from the page itself.

Screenwriters

Pressure-test a character before the rewrite. Find out what they'd do in the scene you haven't written yet.

Directors

Walk into prep knowing why each character makes each choice, with the pages to point to.

Actors

Ask your character what they want, what they know at this point in the story, and what they'd never say.

Producers

Get a deep read on the people in a script, past the logline and the coverage.

Not a normal chatbot

A chatbot plays a part. Your character answers from the page.

Any chatbot can pretend to be someone. It will be agreeable, it will be nice, and it will make things up. Conscious Screenplay answers the way your character would, from what's written, and shows you exactly where that answer came from.

A normal chatbot
Elena: "If I leave right now, are you coming with me?"
As Marcus, I think I'd realize what really matters. I'd cash out, take Elena's hand and walk away from the table together. Love is worth more than any bet!
Sounds nice. It isn't Marcus. No scene, no line, no page. You can't check it, and it contradicts the script.

Three kinds of answer

It knows what it knows, and it says so.

Every question gets read before it's answered, so you always know what kind of answer you're getting.

What happened

"What does the fifty grand pay for?"

Facts come straight from the pages. If it isn't in the script, they won't invent it.

Why

"Why the watch?"

The character's own reading of what they did, with the moments it rests on laid out beside it.

What would you do

"If I leave right now…?"

Scenes that haven't been written. The answer is labeled as a prediction and never passed off as something the script shows.

How it works

Three steps to the conversation.

1

Import

Bring in a screenplay as a PDF, Final Draft, Fountain, Word or plain text file.

2

Pick a character

Choose anyone with a line, and choose where you are in the story.

3

Ask

They answer in their own voice. Open the trail to see the pages it came from.

You make the call

Every answer is a draft.

Nothing a character says becomes canon until you approve it. Keep what rings true, fix what's close, and throw out what isn't them.

.pdf.fdx.fountain.docx.txt
Approve

The line becomes canon for that character, and it comes back the same way next time you ask.

Edit

Rewrite it in your words. Your version is the one that stands.

Reject

That answer is never returned again. Ask once more and it's reasoned fresh, with that one set aside.

Questions

Before you ask them.

Is this a chatbot pretending to be a character?

No. A chatbot plays a part and fills the gaps with whatever sounds good. Here, every answer comes from what the character says and does on the page, and it shows you the lines and page numbers it stands on.

Will it make things up?

Facts come only from the script. When you ask about a moment that isn't written, the answer is labeled as a prediction and never passed off as something the script shows. Where the script is silent, it tells you.

Does a character know how the story ends?

Only if you let them. You pick the story point, and the character knows only what has happened up to there.

Does it change the script?

No. Nothing a character says becomes part of the story until you approve it, and you can rewrite any line in your own words.

What does it run on?

Conscious Screenplay is a desktop app for Windows and Mac.

What formats does it read?

PDF, Final Draft (.fdx), Fountain, Word (.docx) and plain text.
